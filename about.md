I am a fourth-year Mechanical Engineering major with a minor in Computer Science at the University of Florida, with career interests in manufacturing and product design. I enjoy working at the intersection of design and fabrication—taking concepts from CAD to manufactured parts while considering real-world constraints such as tooling, tolerances, and assembly.

My technical background includes experience with SolidWorks through UF coursework and Design and Manufacturing Lab, and extensive use of Fusion 360 for its integrated CAM capabilities. I regularly use Python for small projects and tooling, including a card game implementation and a parametric question generator for Design and Manufacturing Lab. I am also familiar with Creality, Prusa, and Bambu slicers through a variety of 3D-printing projects, giving me experience across multiple additive manufacturing workflows.

In addition to my technical training, I have held leadership roles that have shaped how I approach collaboration, planning, and mentorship. I have been involved in the Vietnamese Eucharistic Youth Movement since childhood and currently serve as a youth leader, where I teach structured lessons, mentor younger students, and support community volunteer efforts. These experiences have strengthened my ability to communicate complex ideas clearly and guide groups toward shared goals.

I have also remained involved in performance-based organizations that emphasize coordination and disciplined execution. After serving as colorguard captain during my senior year of high school, I began teaching colorguard and currently teach at Gainesville High School, instructing students in technique and performance. Earlier in my teaching experience, I worked with Santa Fe High School, where the team finished third at the state championships despite limited resources—an outcome that reinforced the importance of adaptability, iterative improvement, and teamwork.

At the University of Florida, I am currently a member of Ace Dance Company, a student-run organization that performs at campus and external events. I previously served as Performance Director for the Vietnamese Student Organization (VSO), where I coordinated large-scale performances for events such as AASA, Tet Trung Thu, and Tet. This role involved selecting and managing choreographers, coordinating practice schedules for over 100 dancers, and collaborating with leaders from other organizations to minimize conflicts. I am also currently the stroll coordinator for Pi Delta Psi Fraternity, where I organize and lead performance practices within the multicultural community. These roles require systems-level planning, communication, and accountability—skills that directly translate to engineering project management.

My interest in manufacturing was solidified through Design and Manufacturing Lab, where I learned design for manufacturability and produced air engine components using manual machining processes. After shadowing teaching assistants over the summer, I became a teaching assistant for the course and currently serve as a DML TA, overseeing lab sections, instructing students on mill and lathe operations, and providing feedback on technical reports.
